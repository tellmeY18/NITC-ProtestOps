## 3. Defamation

Indian defamation law covers both civil and criminal claims. The defences that matter here are **truth** and **fair comment**.

- **Truth:** the statement is factually accurate and can be proved in court. This is why every claim has a source and a locator.
- **Fair comment:** the statement is an opinion on a matter of public interest, based on true facts, and expressed without malice.

The decks are built to stay inside both:

- facts are attributed to a document,
- opinions are clearly editorial (headlines, red bars, "what it adds up to"),
- reported speech is labelled,
- paraphrases are not put in quotation marks,
- named people are public officials acting in their public role,
- the right of reply is public.

This does not make the project immune. It makes it defensible.

## 4. Privacy

Indian law recognises a right to privacy (including after *K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India*). The repository therefore:

- publishes no student names, roll numbers, phone numbers, emails, hostel details or disciplinary records,
- publishes no private correspondence without consent,
- publishes no faces,
- limits named individuals to public officials and public acts.

See `SECURITY.md` and `docs/privacy.md`.

## 6. Images

No photographs, no video stills, no logos. The illustrations are original line drawings. If you add an image:

- you must own it or have a licence,
- you must have consent for any identifiable person,
- you must record the source and licence in `sources.yml`,
- you must not add it to a deck that names a person without checking the context.

## 7. QR codes and links

The QR codes point to public source lists. Do not point a QR code at:

- a file you do not have the right to distribute,
- a page that collects personal data,
- a link shortener you do not control,
- a private drive or chat.

Archive the target (Wayback Machine) and record the archive URL in `sources.wiki`.

## 8. Takedown and right of reply

If you are named and want a correction or a reply, see `SECURITY.md` and `docs/right-of-reply.md`. The process is:

1. acknowledge within 72 hours,
2. check the claim against the source,
3. correct if wrong,
4. publish the reply if relevant,
5. record the exchange.

## 9. Jurisdiction

The project is published from India and is intended to comply with Indian law. It makes no representation about compliance in other jurisdictions. If you mirror it elsewhere, you are responsible for local law.

## 10. Contributions

By contributing, you confirm that:

- you wrote the contribution, or have the right to submit it,
- it does not contain personal data you do not have consent to publish,
- it does not infringe a third party's copyright,
- you license it under GPL-3.0-or-later.

## 11. If you receive a legal notice

1. Do not publish the notice or the correspondence.
2. Preserve the evidence: the source, the locator, the date, the draft.
3. Tell the maintainers immediately.
4. Do not delete the post until the maintainers have assessed it. Deleting can look like an admission and destroys the record.
5. Consult a lawyer. This document is not a substitute.
