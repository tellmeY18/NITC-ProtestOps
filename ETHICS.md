# Ethics

This project publishes criticism of a public institution and of named public officials. That is legitimate. It is also easy to do badly. These are the lines we hold.

## 1. Public power, public scrutiny

We scrutinise **institutions and the people who hold public office in them**, acting in that role. A Dean's written reply, a Director's decision, a Senate minute, a public social-media post by an office-holder — these are fair game.

We do not scrutinise private students. We do not name complainants, witnesses, hostel residents, or people who appear in a document only because they were processed by it.

## 2. The claim must be stronger than the anger

Anger is a design choice. Accuracy is a precondition. Every factual sentence on a slide must survive the test in [`FACTCHECK.md`](FACTCHECK.md). If it does not, it is cut, weakened, or labelled as opinion.

## 3. Quote exactly, or do not quote

- **Verbatim:** quotation marks, exact wording, named document and locator.
- **Paraphrase:** no quotation marks, and the copy says it is a summary.
- **Reported:** "according to X", with X named.
- **Inference:** labelled as our reading, not as fact.
- **Opinion:** visibly editorial, never dressed as a finding.

A paraphrase in quotation marks is the single most common way a good argument becomes a defensible lawsuit.

## 4. No personal data, ever

The raw Senate and BoG caches contain roll numbers, phone numbers, emails and disciplinary cases. They stay local. The published repo contains clauses and public statements, not people's files.

If a document is public but contains personal data, we quote **the clause**, not the annexure. If the clause cannot be understood without the annexure, we describe the annexure and do not reproduce it.

## 5. Consent and faces

The design system uses no photographs of people. This is deliberate: a face turns an argument about a decision into an argument about a person, and it invites harassment we cannot control. Illustrations, type and documents are enough.

## 6. Right of reply

Every published deck carries a source panel. Every correction path is public. Before publishing a claim that names a person, we make a good-faith attempt to put the claim to them, or to the office they hold, and we record that attempt.

If they reply, we publish the reply — in the deck, in `outputs/<post>/CORRECTIONS.md`, or both. A right of reply is not a concession. It is what separates criticism from a pile-on.

## 7. Corrections are not failures

We correct quickly, visibly and without arguing. A corrected claim with a note is more credible than a deleted post. The correction log is part of the work, not an embarrassment.

## 8. Do not become the thing you are criticising

- No doxxing.
- No speculation about someone's family, health, caste, religion or sexuality.
- No coordinated harassment.
- No fabricated documents, forged screenshots, or "edited for clarity" quotes.
- No publishing a private message without the sender's consent, unless it is a threat or evidence of a public act and the public interest clearly outweighs the privacy cost — and then only with the identifying details minimised.

## 9. Protect your sources

Assume anything you post can be screenshotted, archived and used against the person who gave it to you. Strip metadata. Do not name a source who asked not to be named. Do not describe a source so precisely that they become identifiable. Keep raw material in `private/`, never in git.

## 10. The test

Before publishing, ask:

> Would I be comfortable defending this sentence, with this evidence, in front of the person it names — and in front of a student who disagrees with me?

If not, it is not ready.
